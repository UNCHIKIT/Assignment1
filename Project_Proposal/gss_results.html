How to read this (honest summary): within any given year the income–happiness gradient is large, significant by every metric (χ² p≈2.4e-150; OR = 2.48, CI [2.23, 2.75]; Cohen's d = 0.57, CI [0.52, 0.61]) and survives age/sex/education controls. Across time the picture splits: from 1972 to 2012 happiness was statistically flat (Kendall τ on year means: τ = −0.24, p = 0.56) while GDP per capita rose ~2× — a richer America, no happier. The sharp 2022–24 drop is significant in the era tests but overlaps the GSS switch to mixed-mode collection (toggle in chart 2) and the pandemic era. Caveats: (1) income is stored in coarse bands (25 rungs), so we rank within year instead of trusting cross-era dollar values; (2) cross-sectional association ≠ causation (health, marriage, and personality correlate with both); (3) survey happiness is self-reported. Bottom line: money buys happiness relative to your fellow Americans; the nation's doubling of income bought none.
